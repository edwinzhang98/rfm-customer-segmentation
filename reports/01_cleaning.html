01 · Data cleaning and checks¶

Purpose: show that the transaction, customer and article files are complete and correctly understood, and document how every messy part was handled before any customer is scored.

Pipeline steps covered: sql/00_stage_raw.sql → sql/01_data_quality_checks.sql → sql/02–04 (clean marts) → sql/13_reconciliation_checks.sql. This notebook only reads their outputs.

TL;DR¶

  • The files are complete and the totals tie out. 31,788,324 transaction rows, 1,371,980 customers and 105,542 articles, exactly the published counts. Of 23 automated checks, 16 pass and 7 are known issues with a documented handling; none fail. All 19 reconciliation checks agree to the cent from raw rows to segments.
  • Repeated rows are extra units, not duplicates. There is no quantity column: 2,974,905 rows (9.4%) repeat another row exactly, up to 569 identical T-shirts bought by one customer in a store on one day. Deleting them would remove 9.7% of revenue, so they are collapsed into a units column instead.
  • Money is reconstructed. Price × 590 lands on a whole cent for every row and on familiar shelf prices (78% of items end in .99; 9.99, 19.99 and 14.99 alone are 32% of items). Money is reported in CU (currency not disclosed) and is gross demand: the file has no returns.
  • Channel 1 = store, 2 = online, confirmed by the COVID closure: channel 1 has no sales from 2020-03-20 to 2020-05-05 (9 stray rows in 47 days) and comes back when stores reopen on 2020-05-06, while online orders rise.
  • 864 accounts go on a Review List (bulk or near-daily buying, up to purchases on 427 of 734 days). They stay in every total but get no offers or paid media until CRM / retail ops have reviewed them; they are 0.5% of revenue, so no result depends on them.

Question¶

Before 1.36M customers are segmented and a campaign is sized on them: can we trust the data?

  1. Are the files complete, and are the keys what we assume?
  2. What do the unlabelled parts mean (repeated rows, scaled prices, channel 1 vs 2)?
  3. Which records need a rule before scoring (odd prices, gaps in the customer file, accounts that do not buy like a typical consumer)?
  4. Do units, revenue and customers add up at every step of the pipeline?

Data¶

Method¶

  1. Compare staged row counts with the counts published with the dataset; test keys and referential integrity.
  2. For each part of the data that needs interpretation (repeated rows, prices, channel, order definition), show the evidence, the decision and what it changes in revenue.
  3. Profile the gaps in the customer file and the accounts with extreme buying patterns.
  4. Close with the full automated check table (sql/01) and the reconciliation table (sql/13). Status: PASS = as expected; FLAG = known issue, handled as described; FAIL = stops the pipeline.

def show_sql(file: str, start: str, end: str, before: int = 0) -> None:
    """Print part of a SQL file: from the line containing `start` (plus `before` lines above) to the next line containing `end`."""
    lines = (get_paths().sql / file).read_text().splitlines()
    i = next(n for n, line in enumerate(lines) if start in line)
    j = next(n for n in range(i, len(lines)) if end in lines[n])
    where = f"line {j + 1}" if i - before == j else f"lines {i - before + 1}-{j + 1}"
    print(f"-- sql/{file}, {where}\n")
    print("\n".join(lines[i - before:j + 1]))


def table(df: pd.DataFrame, ints=(), pcts=(), digits: dict | None = None, pct_digits: int = 1):
    """Show a frame without its index: thousands separators, percentages, text left-aligned."""
    fmt = {c: "{:,.0f}" for c in ints} | {c: f"{{:.{pct_digits}%}}" for c in pcts} | (digits or {})
    text_cols = [c for c in df.columns if pd.api.types.is_string_dtype(df[c]) or df[c].dtype == object]
    styled = df.style.format(fmt, na_rep="-", escape="html").hide(axis="index")
    if not text_cols:
        return styled
    heads = [{"selector": f"th.col{df.columns.get_loc(c)}", "props": "text-align: left;"} for c in text_cols]
    return styled.set_properties(subset=text_cols, **{"text-align": "left"}).set_table_styles(heads, overwrite=False)

2. Repeated rows are extra units of the same item¶

There is no quantity column, so two identical T-shirts bought together appear as two identical rows. The table counts how many identical copies each distinct row has.

4. Very low prices and prices far from the article's usual price¶

Some items sell for a few cents, or far below or above the article's median price. fct_sales_lines keeps each line's price, an is_low_price flag (< 0.50 CU) and the ratio to the article's median price.

8. Accounts that do not buy like a typical consumer → Review List¶

A few accounts buy dozens of identical items at once or shop on 150 or more different days in two years. Staff, resellers or business accounts are possible explanations, but the data cannot confirm any of them, so the accounts are not labelled: they go on a Review List. The flag column in the marts is still called is_excluded; nothing is removed from any total.

Limitations¶

  • No returns. Revenue is gross demand. Returns typically hit online orders harder than store purchases, so any comparison of online and store value flatters online customers until returns data is joined.
  • No order id. Order = customer × day × channel. Several online orders on one day count as one (fewer, bigger online orders than in reality); a store visit plus an online order on the same day count as two (1.0% of customer-days).
  • Store purchases are only visible when the shopper identifies as a member. Store activity is understated, and "online only" means "no identified store purchase", not "never visits a store".
  • COVID year. No store sales from 2020-03-20 to 2020-05-05; stores reopened 2020-05-06. The 12 months behind the current scores include the closure, so some store customers may look less recent and less frequent because stores were shut, not because they lost interest.
  • Data starts on 2018-09-20. A customer's first order in the data is not always their first order ever, so tenure is a lower bound.
  • The Review List is a pattern, not an identity. The three triggers are behavioural thresholds; who these accounts are needs to be confirmed by CRM / retail ops.

Recommendations / next step¶

  • Build all scoring on fct_sales_lines → fct_orders → dim_customers (reconciled above), never on raw rows, and keep money in integer cents of CU.
  • Send the 864 Review List accounts to CRM / retail ops for review; until then they stay in totals and out of offers and paid media.
  • Ask e-commerce / Finance for returns by channel, so value comparisons between online and store customers can be restated net of returns.
  • Re-run sql/01 and sql/13 with every data refresh; any FAIL stops the pipeline.
  • Next: notebooks/02_rfm.ipynb (RFM scoring and segment rules).
